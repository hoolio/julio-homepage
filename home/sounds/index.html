What I'm listening to this week.

01 / The Patient
Deconstructed Ambient

Debit

The Long Count

Delia Beatriz makes music under the name Debit out of Mexico City and Brooklyn. Her album The Long Count reworked flutes recorded from pre-Columbian instruments at the Mayan Sound Atlas into something closer to deconstructed ambient than club music.

It is patient. It does not flinch. You can hear the original instruments breathing beneath the digital processing — not sampled, not mimicked, but held.

Listen on Bandcamp
02 / The Hollow
Regional Mexican · Club

El Plvybxy

Cumbia at the Edge

El Plvybxy produces a kind of regional Mexican club that feels hollowed-out and haunted. Cumbia's DNA shows through but the scaffolding has been torn off.

You can hear the machine deciding. There's a moment midway through each track where you realize it stopped being about rhythm and started being about memory.

Find on Bandcamp
03 / The Structural
Tribal · Reggaeton

DJ Fucci

Monterrey Mixes

DJ Fucci has been putting out mixes that lean into tribal percussion and reggaeton rebuilt as a structural practice. Rooted somewhere between Monterrey and the internet.

These aren't sets — they're arguments. Each mix walks you through a specific proposition about what percussion can still do.

Follow on SoundCloud
04 / The Excavation
Sound Design · Club

El Irreal Veintiuno

Jose Cruz

El Irreal Veintiuno — Jose Cruz — is making the stuff that sounds, to me, the most like the future. Sound design as excavation.

Something about it feels right for the moment. Like he's recovered a signal that was always there and is only now learning to transmit it cleanly.
