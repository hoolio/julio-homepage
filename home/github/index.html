The Arc

I joined GitHub in December 2012. It had just raised its Series A at a $750M valuation, had fewer than 100 employees, and about three million users. By the time I left in 2018, the company was acquired by Microsoft for $7.5 billion, and had become the place where software gets made.

I came in as the first general counsel. Over six years, I grew into Chief Business, Strategy & Operations Officer — overseeing legal, policy, people, partnerships, marketing, and communications. Built the company out of the nineties idea of a legal department and into something like an open-source legal practice: lawyers wrote in Markdown, collaborated in pull requests, and shipped policy like it was code.

The Acquisition

The Microsoft acquisition closed in October 2018. It was the largest all-stock acquisition of a private company at the time. I was in the room for the negotiation and helped land the deal alongside Chris Wanstrath and Nat Friedman.

"A little eccentricity in your company is probably not a bad thing provided you can back it up with the constellation of things that normal companies also have."
