In 2006 we had a room at the Office Ops Arts & Community Center in Bushwick. It was a rooftop space, then a warehouse, then an art collective, then a thing that used to be there. We recorded a radio show in that room for the first four months of the show's life, and we called it Choplogic.

::audio[Country Teasers — Boycott the Studio (Live)](https://archive.org/download/choplogic-radio/chop45a.mp3)

There is a specific sound to a record being pulled from a milk crate at 11pm on a Sunday night in a building that used to be something else. A soft percussive thud, a scrape, a held breath. The needle drops. Whoever was in the room went quiet. That was the show.

::pullquote[Broadcasting is a generous kind of selfishness — you do it to be heard, but also because the alternative is silence.]

I lived a block away. Sonali lived three blocks away. The L train was unreliable and we were broke and the internet was still mostly weird. You uploaded an MP3 and then you waited — sometimes days — for the iTunes podcast feed to refresh. People in Germany and Japan and Mexico City were listening, which seemed impossible and also obvious.

## What the show taught me

The show taught me that taste is a practice, not a possession. You don't have it — you do it, episode after episode, and the doing is the thing. It also taught me that curation is a form of writing. Every playlist is an argument.

::break

In the twenty years since, I've run companies and written legal briefs and raised children and watched the internet become a thing I barely recognize. But every once in a while — usually late at night — I put on one of the old episodes and I am back in that rooftop room in Bushwick, surrounded by a particular kind of hope that hadn't been mass-manufactured yet.

::audio[Dan Deacon — Pink Batman (2007)](https://archive.org/download/choplogic-radio/chop44a.mp3)

The archive is back online as of last week. 200 files, 6.7 gigabytes, 74 episodes. Internet Archive is hosting it permanently. I still own chopradio.com. The room is gone, but the recordings survived — on hard drives, then on CRATEZ, then on a Mac I keep meaning to throw away.

Something about this feels like finishing a long sentence I started twenty years ago.
