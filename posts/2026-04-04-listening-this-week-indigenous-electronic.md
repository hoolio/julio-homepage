A corner of experimental electronic music is doing something worth paying attention to right now. Producers across Mexico, Texas, and further south are folding pre-Hispanic sound, field recordings, broken club percussion, and deconstructed dembow into something that sounds like it's being unearthed rather than composed.

Not nostalgia. Not pastiche. <em>A conversation across time.</em>

::break

## Debit

**Delia Beatriz** makes music under the name Debit out of Mexico City and Brooklyn. Her album _The Long Count_ reworked flutes recorded from pre-Columbian instruments at the Mayan Sound Atlas into something closer to deconstructed ambient than club music. It is patient. It does not flinch.

## El Plvybxy

**El Plvybxy** produces a kind of regional Mexican club that feels hollowed-out and haunted. Cumbia's DNA shows through but the scaffolding has been torn off. You can hear the machine deciding.

## DJ Fucci

**DJ Fucci** has been putting out mixes that lean into tribal percussion and reggaeton rebuilt as a structural practice. Rooted somewhere between Monterrey and the internet.

## El Irreal Veintiuno

**El Irreal Veintiuno** — Jose Cruz — is making the stuff that sounds, to me, the most like the future. Sound design as excavation. Something about it feels right for the moment.

::break

I'm not going to pretend to have a thesis here. I'm listening, I'm buying records, I'm sending things to friends. When you hear music that has this much care in it you understand that something is being restored and something new is being made, and those turn out to have always been the same work.
