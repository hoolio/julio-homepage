<h2 id="slide-1--opening-smiths-pin-factory-restated"><a href="#slide-1--opening-smiths-pin-factory-restated">Slide 1 — Opening: "Smith's pin factory, restated"</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line hook:</strong> <em>"In 1776 Adam Smith made a parable out of pins. It wasn't about productivity. It was about what happens when expert judgment becomes a property of the system instead of the artisan."</em></li>
<li><strong>Bullets on slide:</strong>
<ul>
<li>The pin factory was an epistemological claim, not an efficiency one</li>
<li>The factory knew how to make pins; no individual worker did</li>
<li>250 years later, this is happening to cognitive work</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Talk track:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Set the register: this is not "AI is going to be big." It's a structural claim about which industrial revolution we're in.</li>
<li>Promise: a heuristic, a matrix, then the map.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> Stark text slide. Optional: an 18th-century engraving of a pin factory, low contrast, behind the text.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 2 min</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="slide-2--three-industrial-transitions"><a href="#slide-2--three-industrial-transitions">Slide 2 — Three Industrial Transitions</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"Three transitions. Same arc. Different layer of human capability."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> A 3-row table.</li>
</ul>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Transition</th>
<th>What is industrialized</th>
<th>Characteristic infrastructure</th>
<th>Characteristic commodity</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>First (1760–1850)</td>
<td>Muscular force</td>
<td>Mills, foundries, railways</td>
<td>Pig iron, kilowatts</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Second (1940–2010)</td>
<td>Symbol manipulation</td>
<td>Mainframes, internet, cloud</td>
<td>CPU cycles, packets</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Third (2017– )</td>
<td><strong>Cognitive judgment</strong></td>
<td>Foundation labs, agent fabric</td>
<td><strong>Tokens, inferences, decisions</strong></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<ul>
<li><strong>Talk track key lines:</strong>
<ul>
<li>"Each transition's commodity is the prior transition's craft."</li>
<li>"The blacksmith's craft became the foundry's commodity. The bookkeeper's craft became the spreadsheet's commodity. The associate attorney's craft is becoming the foundation model's commodity."</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> Clean three-row table. Highlight row three.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 2 min</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="slide-3--the-triadic-structure"><a href="#slide-3--the-triadic-structure">Slide 3 — The Triadic Structure</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"Every industrial transition runs on three forces. They are running on cognition right now."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> Three concept boxes:
<ul>
<li><strong>Specialization</strong> — Smith → Durkheim → Braverman. Decomposes expert work into discrete operations. Currently: junior associate, junior physician, junior analyst.</li>
<li><strong>Commodification</strong> — Marx → Polanyi. Renders operations into fungible market inputs. Currently: per-token inference, metered cognition, fab-economic foundation labs.</li>
<li><strong>Black-boxing</strong> — Latour → Pasquale → Burrell. Encapsulates capability behind interfaces that hide complexity. Currently: foundation models, by structural design, not engineering accident.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Talk track key lines:</strong>
<ul>
<li>"The black box is not a bug. It's the condition of the system being usable. The factory was a black box too."</li>
<li>"But every black box that produces consequential outputs creates a market for inspection."</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> Three vertical boxes side-by-side. Each with one philosopher's name and one current-cycle example.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 2 min</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="slide-4--the-chain"><a href="#slide-4--the-chain">Slide 4 — The Chain</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"This is the trajectory. The interesting question is where, along it, durable value accrues."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> A single horizontal arrow chain:</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p>tacit expert judgment → codified procedure → model-mediated workflow → system of record / system of action → commoditized output</p>
</blockquote>
<ul>
<li>Underneath, three highlighted positions of capture:
<ol>
<li><strong>System layer</strong> (codification → workflow): the OS-position of the third transition</li>
<li><strong>Trust layer</strong> (workflow → consequential deployment): the apparatus that wraps the black box</li>
<li><strong>Vertical layer</strong> (workflow → professional domain): institutional integration in regulated work</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li><strong>Talk track key lines:</strong>
<ul>
<li>"Don't ask who uses AI. Everyone will. Ask who owns the layer where scarce expertise becomes reusable infrastructure."</li>
<li>"Three positions are structurally privileged. The matrix on the next slide is how we score them."</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> One horizontal arrow with five labels. Three callouts above the arrow showing the privileged capture positions.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 2 min</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="slide-5--the-seven-factor-matrix"><a href="#slide-5--the-seven-factor-matrix">Slide 5 — The Seven-Factor Matrix</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"Seven factors. Each maps to one element of the triadic structure. Each company gets a 1–5 score."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> Two columns.
<ul>
<li>Left: the seven factors with one-line definitions:
<ol>
<li><strong>Cognitive Externalization</strong> — encodes expert work into systems</li>
<li><strong>Workflow Ownership</strong> — sits inside recurring work, not one-off</li>
<li><strong>Epistemic Trust / Auditability</strong> — inspectable, citable, traceable</li>
<li><strong>System-of-Action</strong> — executes, doesn't just recommend</li>
<li><strong>Data / Feedback Loop</strong> — usage compounds advantage</li>
<li><strong>Commoditization Resistance</strong> — hard for the labs to crush</li>
<li><strong>Budget Gravity</strong> — attaches to large existing spend</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li>Right: a small radar chart (or a horizontal bar chart) of an exemplar (Harvey or Sierra) showing the seven scores.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Talk track key lines:</strong>
<ul>
<li>"Aggregate score matters. The shape matters more."</li>
<li>"A 28 with 5s on workflow and budget but a 2 on commoditization resistance is a different investment than a 28 with 5s on resistance and budget."</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> Two-column. The exemplar radar chart is the visual anchor.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 3 min</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="slide-6--the-reformulated-heuristic"><a href="#slide-6--the-reformulated-heuristic">Slide 6 — The Reformulated Heuristic</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"The matrix collapses to one sentence."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> Block-quote on the slide:</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p>The strongest AI investment is in a firm that <strong>captures an expensive cognitive workflow</strong>, <strong>embeds itself in the system of record or system of action</strong>, <strong>compounds through proprietary feedback</strong>, and <strong>preserves enough epistemic trust</strong> that regulated or fiduciary institutions can rely on it.</p>
<p>The weakest is a thin presentation layer over a generic model.</p>
<p>The strongest wedge is <strong>industrialized cognition with inspectability</strong>.</p>
</blockquote>
<ul>
<li>Below: three pathways in declining cycle attractiveness:
<ol>
<li>Own the system layer</li>
<li>Preserve interpretability and control</li>
<li>Verticalize cognition</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li>Avoid: undifferentiated wrappers around frontier models on generic knowledge work.</li>
<li><strong>Talk track:</strong>
<ul>
<li>This is the slide to slow down on. Read the three highlighted phrases out loud.</li>
<li>"Industrialized cognition with inspectability" is the line that lands.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Visual:</strong> Mostly text. Bold the three highlighted phrases in the quote.</li>
<li><strong>Time:</strong> 2 min</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="slide-12--hot-takes-highlighted-five"><a href="#slide-12--hot-takes-highlighted-five">Slide 12 — Hot Takes (Highlighted Five)</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"Five contested positions held with conviction."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> Five short statements, no elaboration on slide:
<ol>
<li><strong>Foundation-lab winners are already chosen.</strong> The middle of the pack will not survive the next training cycle.</li>
<li><strong>"Agent platform" as a stand-alone category will compress.</strong> Defensible agent-infra is cross-platform and policy-governed: payments, identity, eval, audit.</li>
<li><strong>Coding will not consolidate to one winner.</strong> IDE-native, autonomous-task, AI-prototyping, and lab-native are different products. Each produces a category-defining firm.</li>
<li><strong>The trust layer is the most undervalued category in the cycle.</strong> Observability/eval/audit firms are priced as developer tools. They should be priced as financial-services software.</li>
<li><strong>The black-box critique is permanent, not temporary.</strong> Treat regulatory posture as permanent market structure, not transient burden.</li>
</ol>
</li>
<li><strong>Talk track:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Hit each in 30 seconds. Don't elaborate; let them spark Q&#x26;A.</li>
<li>The fourth take is the bridge to the next slide.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="slide-13--the-white-space-an-inspectable-runtime-for-cognitive-work"><a href="#slide-13--the-white-space-an-inspectable-runtime-for-cognitive-work">Slide 13 — The White Space: An Inspectable Runtime for Cognitive Work</a></h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>One-line:</strong> <em>"This is the position the combined Sequoia+Thrive book is missing. The matrix scores it 32–34 out of 35."</em></li>
<li><strong>Slide content:</strong> Define the inspectable runtime by five properties:
<ol>
<li>Agents reason, execute, and write to systems of record from inside it</li>
<li>Intermediate state is <strong>visible and reproducible</strong> — chain of inference, tools called, data fetched, assumptions made</li>
